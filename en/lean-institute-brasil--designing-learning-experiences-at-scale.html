ORGANIZATIONAL CONTEXT

For more than 13 years, I helped shape how knowledge, events, publications and digital experiences were presented at the Brazilian affiliate of the Lean movement. Starting as a graphic designer, I evolved into a multidisciplinary leadership role responsible for visual systems, learning experiences and communication strategies across multiple channels.

THE CHALLENGE

Making Lean Thinking Accessible at Scale

Lean thinking combines management concepts that are not commonly part of a visual artist's background with the disciplines of operational excellence and organizational transformation.

The challenge was not simply to communicate information.

The challenge was to make sophisticated ideas understandable, engaging and actionable across events, books, websites, training programs and marketing initiatives.

At the same time, the organization needed to maintain visual consistency while growing its portfolio of products, publications and large-scale conferences.

THE OPPORTUNITY

Building a Unified Experience Ecosystem

Rather than treating each communication piece as an isolated project, I proposed a unified experience ecosystem.

Every touchpoint would reinforce the same principles:

  • clarity
  • simplicity
  • visual consistency
  • cognitive accessibility
  • alignment with Lean philosophy

This approach connected physical events, digital platforms, publications and promotional materials into a coherent experience.

DESIGN APPROACH

Translating Lean into Visual Language

Lean principles emphasize clarity, efficiency and the elimination of unnecessary complexity.

These same principles guided the visual and interaction design decisions.
The goal was to reduce cognitive friction and help users focus on what mattered most: learning and applying knowledge.

Information Architecture Inspired by Classical Rhetoric

For the 2021 website redesign, I structured content around three Aristotelian principles that guided credibility, engagement and information flow:

Ethos
(Credibility)

To build trust through evidence, expertise and institutional legitimacy.

Pathos
(Engagement)

To create emotional and professional relevance for different audiences.

Logos
(Logic)

To organize information into intuitive pathways for decision-making and action.

This framework helped organize information in a way that increased clarity, trust and usability.

Semiotics and Visual Consistency

The visual language was carefully aligned with the international Lean movement.

Through semiotic principles and consistent use of symbolic elements, the design reinforced organizational identity while making complex concepts easier to navigate and understand.

FEATURED PROJECT

Daily Management to Execute Strategy

One of the most significant editorial projects was the book Daily Management to Execute Strategy.

Working closely with authors Robson Gouveia and José Roberto Ferro, I developed the complete visual system for the publication.
Instead of relying on generic software-generated graphics, every illustration, table and visual framework was custom-designed to improve reader comprehension and better express Lean principles.

The project was developed remotely during the pandemic, requiring intensive collaboration, visual translation of complex concepts and continuous coordination with ongoing institutional demands.
The result was a publication that transformed management concepts into a highly accessible visual learning experience.

IMPACT

Building a National Learning and Communication Ecosystem

Over more than a decade, I helped create a unified communication and learning ecosystem that supported:

  • national conferences
  • professional education programs
  • book launches
  • digital transformation initiatives
  • institutional communication
  • knowledge dissemination at scale

The work connected strategy, design, education and storytelling into a consistent experience that strengthened the organization's presence and influence across Brazil.

The project increased the visibility of Rogério BigBross’s journey, strengthened connections between artists, audiences, and cultural agents, and demonstrated the potential of combining documentary filmmaking, digital storytelling, and community participation.

Beyond documenting a story, Bahia Underground evolved into a space for cultural memory, independent production, and collective mobilization.
