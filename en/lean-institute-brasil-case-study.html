THE CHALLENGE

Making Lean Thinking Accessible at Scale

Lean thinking combines management concepts, operational excellence and organizational transformation principles that are often complex and highly specialized.

The challenge was not simply to communicate information.

The challenge was to communicate management principles in ways that were understandable, engaging and actionable, while maintaining consistency across an expanding portfolio that included conference materials, training programs, publications, websites, online courses, and educational content.

THE OPPORTUNITY

Building a Unified Experience Ecosystem

I designed an integrated communication ecosystem that ensured global brand consistency while connecting conferences, publications, digital platforms, and educational initiatives into a seamless, unified experience.

Every touchpoint was designed around the same principles:

  • clarity
  • simplicity
  • visual consistency
  • cognitive accessibility
  • alignment with Lean philosophy

This approach connected physical events, digital platforms, educational publications and promotional materials into a coherent learning experience.

DESIGN APPROACH

Translating Lean into Visual Language

Lean principles emphasize clarity, efficiency and the elimination of unnecessary complexity.

These same principles guided the visual and interaction design decisions.
The goal was to reduce cognitive friction and help users focus on what mattered most: learning and applying knowledge.

Information Architecture Inspired by Classical Rhetoric

For the 2021 Lean Institute Brasil website redesign, I structured information around three Aristotelian principles:

Ethos
(Credibility)

To build trust through evidence, expertise and institutional legitimacy.

Pathos
(Engagement)

To create emotional and professional relevance for different audiences.

Logos
(Logic)

To organize information into intuitive pathways for learning and decision-making.

This framework helped create an experience that was attractive, functional and efficient.

Visual Simplification

Visual simplification became a direct expression of Lean thinking itself.

Through visual decoding and information hierarchy, I facilitated understanding and helped people focus on the essential principles and practices of Lean thinking.

Semiotics and Visual Consistency

The visual language strengthened differentiation while maintaining a strong connection with the principles established by the Lean Enterprise Institute in the United States.
By applying semiotic principles and using the "leapers" as a central symbolic element, I reinforced visual unity and created a coherent representation of Lean philosophy across digital and physical experiences.

Institutional Website Redesign 2014

Client: Lean Institute Brasil
Project: Institutional Website Redesign 2016
Media: Responsive Website
Role: Creative Direction • Information Architecture • UI Design
Actions: Creative direction, interface redesign, content organization, fixed-width layout design, homepage restructuring and website implementation.

The 2014 redesign evolved the institute's previous website, originally launched in 2009, with the objective of improving the presentation of its expanding portfolio of services while supporting the organization's growing digital communication needs.

Developed in parallel with the Lean Summit 2014 website, the project balanced two independent digital initiatives within the same delivery schedule. The institutional communication strategy called for maximum visibility of the institute's services and benefits on the homepage, while the navigation structure was intentionally kept concise to avoid an overly extensive menu. These constraints required a denser approach to content presentation, with less visual breathing room than would be desirable from a contemporary UX perspective. Within those boundaries, I established a clearer hierarchy and prioritized strategic offerings such as Lean Transformation, professional training programs and the Lean Summit, while maintaining access to the broader institutional portfolio.

The redesign strengthened the visibility of the institute's core business areas and contributed to new business opportunities, while providing a more structured foundation for the organization's subsequent digital evolution.

Daily Management to Execute Strategy

Client: Lean Institute Brasil
Project: Gerenciamento Diário — Daily Management to Execute Strategy
Media: Book • Editorial Publication
Role: Creative Direction • Editorial Design • Art Direction
Actions: Complete visual system development, original illustration, custom diagrams and charts, information design, book design, editorial layout and print production supervision.

The Gerenciamento Diário publication was conceived and developed as a complete visual system from the ground up. Working closely with the authors, I translated complex Lean management concepts into an original visual language, developing illustrations, diagrams and visual frameworks from concept through final artwork.

Achieving the desired level of conceptual and visual precision required an ongoing dialogue with the authors through reviews and working sessions. This collaborative process was essential to ensure that each visual accurately reflected how Daily Management is practiced across different environments—from construction sites and offices to hospitals, agriculture and other operational contexts.

The resulting publication reached beyond its original Brazilian edition and was subsequently adapted for other organizations within the Lean Global Network, including English and Spanish editions. This represented an interesting reversal of the localization process I had applied across more than 40 other publications, which were primarily adapted from English into Portuguese. In this case, the visual and editorial system originated in Brazil and was carried forward into other languages and markets.

The international editions provide a tangible record of this process, demonstrating how the visual and editorial system developed for the Brazilian publication was extended to new linguistic and cultural contexts while preserving its core identity and conceptual clarity.
